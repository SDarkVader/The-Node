# Proposed reputation loss events for owner review

These are proposals, not current rules or evidence that the actions below exist in the society action menu. The owner requested review before choosing loss events. Continue to use the PR #60 signed scoring model, with its illustrative parameters, only after the necessary event provenance and outcome rules are defined.

| Candidate | Evidence needed before a negative event | Exclusions and open decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmed harmful deed | A kernel-recorded harmful act, its affected person or public asset, and a separately verified attribution to the actor. | An accusation, rumour, private impression or mere proximity is not proof. Decide which harmful acts exist and which witnesses or authoritative records establish attribution. |
| Unfulfilled explicit obligation | A kernel-recorded obligation voluntarily accepted by a person, a defined deadline and a verified failure to perform it when performance was possible. | Do not count a provider failure, safety stop, unavailable resources, an unaccepted task or an incomplete run as misconduct. Define obligations and exceptions before implementation. |
| Verified dishonest transaction | A recorded commitment and actual transaction outcome that demonstrate a specific breach by the same person. | Low prices, failed orders, ordinary supply shortages and another inhabitant's belief are not evidence of dishonesty. Define transactions and defenses before implementation. |

Each event would need a stable unique ID, persistent subject, event day, public or private disclosure rule and agreed weight; repeated observations of one deed must not create repeated penalties. The proposed reducer has separate positive and negative same-day caps, but those experimental parameters are not yet calibrated as gameplay policy. Determine whether a verified event is public, private or observer-specific before exposing it.

An inactive occupant and a backstopped vacancy are separate questions. The existing routine stops when its turn cap is reached; that stop is not a completed day or evidence of an inhabitant's culpability. Existing vacancies, conscription and performance preference remain in force. A new rule that turns inactivity into vacancy, including its conditions and any opportunity for recovery, requires its own concrete review.
